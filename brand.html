Type, colour and copy rules

One page, so it's short enough to actually follow. Everything on the site and in the CV, deck and email signature should come from here.

Positioning

What this says

B2B marketing for heavy industry, where the buyer is an engineer, the purchase is capital expenditure and the decision takes months. Go-to-market, full-funnel paid social, and technical content that engineers will use.

Three claims sit underneath it. Everything on the site should be evidence for one of them. If a section isn't, cut it.

  • Technical credibility. Reads the specification. Won't publish a claim that hasn't been checked.
  • Commercial seriousness. Agrees the success measure before launch and reports a recommendation rather than a dashboard.
  • Builds systems. Produces the repeatable framework, not the one-off campaign, and automates the manual parts.
Copy rules

Plain, specific, first person

State what the job was, what you did and what happened. No metaphors, no aphorisms, no lines written to be quotable. Detail is what makes writing sound like a person; style is what makes it sound generated.

Do

Open on the concrete thing: a market, a budget, a number. Use short declarative sentences. Include what was hard and what you'd change. Say "I" and say what you decided. British and Singapore spelling throughout.

Don't

Stack three or more deliverables in one comma list. Write a line whose job is to sound clever. Use pull quotes. Reach for game-changing, unlock, revolutionise. Over-use em dashes and semicolons.

The test

Read a paragraph and ask whether a marketer who has never worked in this sector could have written it. If they could, it isn't finished. The defence against sounding machine-generated is detail only you have, not better phrasing.

Typography

One family, weight does the work

Libre Franklin, an open interpretation of Franklin Gothic. A single family across the whole system, with hierarchy carried by weight rather than by mixing faces. It's a newspaper and signage typeface, which suits the subject, and it reads cleanly at small sizes.

Extrabold 800

Headings. Tracking −0.028 to −0.04em, line height under 1.05. This is where the boldness lives, so set it large and tight.

Bold 700 — subheads, labels, table headers

Uppercase labels get 0.1em letter-spacing. Never uppercase anything longer than four words.

Regular 400 — running text, 17 to 19px, line height 1.6, measure capped at 36em. That is about 70 characters, which is the point past which the eye starts losing its place on the return sweep.

Fallbacks for Word, PowerPoint and email: ITC Franklin Gothic or Franklin Gothic if licensed, otherwise Helvetica Neue or Arial. Never substitute a serif.

Colour

One accent, used sparingly

Near-black and off-white do almost all the work. Pink is the only colour that appears regularly. The rest are held in reserve. That restraint is what keeps a pink and mint palette reading as deliberate rather than decorative, which matters when the audience is port engineering and senior industrial management.

#1C0113Ink. Text, dark bands, structural rules.
#EAFDE6Mint. Hero and section bands.
#E01876Pink. Accent bars, underlines, the footer edge.
#CE166DPink, text weight. Small type only. 5.2:1 on paper.
#F077F3Violet. Charts and second series only.
#EDC951Gold. Highlight marks. Never a background.
  • Roughly 70% paper, 20% ink, 7% mint, 3% pink. If pink is doing more than that, something else has stopped working.
  • Full-strength pink is for bars, rules and underlines. As small text it fails contrast on both paper and mint, so text uses the darkened #CE166D. The difference is invisible; the accessibility difference is not.
  • Gold is a highlight behind text and nothing else.
  • In dark mode the accent bars stay pink and text switches to a lifted pink that holds contrast on the dark ground.
Layout

Bars and rules, no boxes

  • The label device. A solid pink bar, 5px tall and about 44px wide, above every section label. It is the one mark that repeats across the whole system and it reproduces in PowerPoint and Canva without effort.
  • Rules, not cards. Three-pixel ink rules open a group, one-pixel rules separate items inside it. No drop shadows, no rounded corners, no pill tags.
  • Full-bleed bands. Mint for the hero and one mid-page section, ink for the footer with a six-pixel pink edge. Colour arrives in blocks rather than in decoration.
  • Asymmetric grid. Sticky label column on the left, content on the right, nothing centred.
  • Numbers set large and heavy. Figures are typography, not badges.

Wordmark

The full name in uppercase Libre Franklin, extrabold on the first word and regular on the second. No monogram and no icon. A well-set name is more credible than a mark, and it avoids the most obvious tell of all, which is an abstract geometric logo that a generator produced.

Applying it

Beyond the site

  • LinkedIn banner. Ink ground, wordmark left in mint, positioning line under it, one pink bar. Nothing else.
  • Slides and one-pagers. Ink title slide, paper content slides, pink on the single number that matters on each page.
  • CV. Same weights, same rules, same numbered structure as the case studies, so it reads as the same document family as the site.
  • Email signature. Name in bold, role under it in grey, one pink rule above the contact block.
